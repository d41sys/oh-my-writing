# structured-drafting eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Fit and scale

1. Was the process sized to the task (a short message got a brief and a draft, not every stage), and were skipped stages left unmentioned rather than padded?
2. If the author said "just write it" or the piece is short, did you write instead of running a question round, and list assumptions at the top?
3. Did you ask at most three questions, and does each answer change the structure?

## Brief

4. Does the brief (shown or held) name one reader, what they already know, and the one thing they should understand, decide or do?
5. Is every guess about the reader, channel or length marked as an assumption rather than stated as fact?

## Material and gaps

6. Is every unit in the draft traceable to supplied material, and are authors' views labelled as such?
7. Is every missing example, number, quote, source, credential or opinion handled as `[ASK AUTHOR: ...]`, `[VERIFY: ...]` or a question, with nothing invented?
8. Are contradictions in the material surfaced to the author, not silently resolved?
9. Is the author's material unchanged on disk (draft written separately)?

## Core, order and grounding

10. Can the core message be stated in one or two sentences, and could a knowledgeable reader reasonably dispute it (not a platitude)?
11. Does every block use only concepts that the reader brings or that an earlier block introduced?
12. Are named or coined terms landed once, with the same word used for the same concept everywhere?
13. Is the order built from what the reader lacks, not from the order in which the author learned things (unless the piece is a narrative of the discovery)?

## Shape and outline

14. Was the shape chosen from the reader's job and purpose, not from the document label, and was a fixed venue or house template respected when one exists?
15. Do the outline lines alone, read in order, tell the story (no bare topic labels like "Background")?
16. Does each block have a stated cost if removed, and were blocks with none cut or merged?
17. Would swapping two body blocks break something (a real through-line), or was the gap reported?
18. Is the weight uneven on purpose, with the most room given to what is new or central?
19. Are keep / cut (with reason) / merge / leftover decisions visible to the author?

## Draft blocks

20. Does each block open with its message (or deliberately hold it for a stated turn), give evidence, and hand off?
21. Are claims, qualifiers, numbers, names and negations the same strength as in the material?
22. Is each block's form (prose, list, table, quote, code) a deliberate choice, and are real lists kept as lists?
23. Does the ending serve the reader's next need, with no stock closer, recap padding or invented conclusion?
24. Were bridges added only where the relation exists in the material?

## Co-author mode (skip if not used)

25. Was the author asked for context in a few targeted questions, with a free dump allowed, instead of a long questionnaire?
26. For each section, did the author curate options (keep / remove with reason / combine) before drafting, and was the section drafted only from kept items?
27. Were edits after feedback local, leaving everything not named unchanged, and were the author's own edits preserved?
28. Was a fresh-reader test run or routed to `independent-review`, and was its limit stated (tests self-sufficiency, not correctness)?

## Hand-off

29. Does the final note say what was not verified and which sibling skill to run next (`evidence-grounded-writing`, `anti-slop-editing`, `author-voice`, `independent-review`)?

## Final read

Read only the outline lines, then the first sentence of each block. Does a reader who lacks your context get the argument and know what to do next? If not, return to stage 4 or 7.
