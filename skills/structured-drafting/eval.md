# structured-drafting eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Fit and scale

1. Was the process sized to the task (a short message got a brief and a draft, not every stage), and were skipped stages left unmentioned rather than padded?
2. If the author said "just write it" or the piece is short, did you write instead of running a question round, and list assumptions in the reply (not in the deliverable)?
3. Did you ask at most three questions, and does each answer change the structure?

## Brief

4. Does the brief (shown or held) name one reader, what they already know, and the one thing they should understand, decide or do?
5. Is every guess about the reader, channel or length marked as an assumption rather than stated as fact?

## Material and gaps

6. Is every unit in the draft traceable to supplied material, and are authors' views labelled as such?
7. Is every missing example, number, quote, source, credential or opinion handled as a plain stated gap or an omitted sentence in the draft, and as a question to the author (marker line in the reply), with nothing invented?
8. Are contradictions in the material surfaced, with both values and who gave each kept in the draft (not left out, not folded into a range), a plain statement that the material does not settle which is right when the author could not be asked, and nothing downstream computed from either?
9. Do tentative, unconfirmed and attributed items keep that status in the draft (nothing decided, confirmed or stated as fact that the material left open)?
10. Is the author's material unchanged on disk (draft written separately)?

## Core, order and grounding

11. Can the core message be stated in one or two sentences, and could a knowledgeable reader reasonably dispute it (not a platitude)?
12. Does every block use only concepts that the reader brings or that an earlier block introduced?
13. Are named or coined terms landed once, with the same word used for the same concept everywhere?
14. Is the order built from what the reader lacks, not from the order in which the author learned things (unless the piece is a narrative of the discovery)?

## Shape and outline

15. Was the shape chosen from the reader's job and purpose, not from the document label, and was a fixed venue or house template respected when one exists?
16. Do the outline lines alone, read in order, tell the story (no bare topic labels like "Background")?
17. Does each block have a stated cost if removed, and were blocks with none cut or merged?
18. Would swapping two body blocks break something (a real through-line), or was the gap reported?
19. Is the weight uneven on purpose, with the most room given to what is new or central?
20. Does the outline cover who is affected and what could go wrong or be objected to, where the material supports it, with the rest recorded as gaps?
21. Are keep / cut (with reason) / merge / leftover decisions visible to the author?

## Draft blocks

22. Does each block open with its message (or deliberately hold it for a stated turn) and give evidence, with a plain bridge only where the next block does not follow on its own?
23. Are claims, qualifiers, numbers (including framing figures such as a position in a schedule, and a stated total or duration beside its endpoints; a short lead or subject line does not excuse dropping them), names, negations and status the same strength as in the material?
24. Is each block's form (prose, list, table, quote, code) a deliberate choice, and are real lists kept as lists?
25. Does the ending serve the reader's next need, with no stock closer, recap padding or invented conclusion? If the shape expects a recommendation or next step that the material lacks, does the draft say so plainly (or leave it out) and the reply carry the question, not a guess?
26. Were bridges added only where the relation exists in the material?
26a. List every reference number, code, abbreviation, tool name and first name in the draft. Does this reader know each one? Each one the reader does not know is replaced by what it is ("the safety approval") or cut, and a true figure the reader does not need for what they do next is gone when the example or the documents this reader knows carry less detail.

## Deliverable file and reader-facing text

27. When a save path was given, does the file hold the draft alone (no label, brief, outline, decision list or note), and only as long as the task size calls for?
28. Is the reader-facing draft free of `[ASK AUTHOR]`, `[VERIFY]` and `[UNTESTED]` markers, `[source: ...]` tags, outline or source comment lines, and process narration ("as requested", "based on your notes"), unless the author asked for a draft to edit?
29. Are gaps stated plainly (a fact about the material) or omitted, with the author's questions in the reply as marker lines, and also in `open-questions.md` beside a saved file when any question is open?

## Co-author mode (skip if not used)

30. Was the author asked for context in a few targeted questions, with a free dump allowed, instead of a long questionnaire?
31. For each section, did the author curate options (keep / remove with reason / combine) before drafting, and was the section drafted only from kept items?
32. Were edits after feedback local, leaving everything not named unchanged, and were the author's own edits preserved?
33. Was a fresh-reader test run or routed to `independent-review`, and was its limit stated (tests self-sufficiency, not correctness)?

## Hand-off

34. Does the final note say what was not verified and which sibling skill to run next (`evidence-grounded-writing`, `anti-slop-editing`, `author-voice`, `independent-review`)?
35. Does the draft read as the author wrote it: no headings in a text under about 400 words or in an email, letter or post, plain transitions with no signposting or analysis labels, prose for fewer than about four items, the author's own person and register, and each caveat or limit stated once at the claim it limits?

## Final read

Read only the outline lines, then the first sentence of each block. Does a reader who lacks your context get the argument and know what to do next? If not, return to stage 4 or 7.
