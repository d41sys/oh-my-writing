# Brief template

Load when: starting a draft that is longer than a short message, or when the author asks what the brief is. The brief is shown to the author in the reply and never goes into the draft. Fill from the request and material first; ask only about fields that would change the structure. Delete fields that do not apply.

```
Working title:
One-line restatement of the task (confirm with the author if the request was vague):

READER
- Who reads this (one real or typical person, not "everyone"):
- What they already know (name the concepts they bring):
- What they lack (the gap the piece closes):
- Their state when they open it (rushed, sceptical, curious, obliged, deciding):

PURPOSE
- The one thing they should understand / decide / do afterwards:
- Core message in one or two sentences (stage 4; may stay blank until the inventory is done):
- What the reader should be able to do or say after reading, in a single sentence:

CONSTRAINTS
- Channel and format (email, post, report, doc, slide notes, README):
- Length or reading time:
- Required sections, house template, venue or platform rules:
- Language and register:
- Deadline and who approves:
- Must include / must avoid (with the reason):

MATERIAL
- Supplied (files, notes, transcripts, links):
- Treated as read-only: yes / no
- Known gaps (examples, numbers, quotes the author must supply):

SCALE
- Task size (tiny / short / standard / large) and stages to run:
- Mode: solo / co-author / explore

ASSUMPTIONS (every guess made in place of a question)
1.
2.

OPEN QUESTIONS (at most three, each changes the structure)
1.
```

## Notes on filling it

- "What they already know" is the field most often left empty and the one that decides the grounding order. If it is unknown, state your assumption and say which blocks would change if it were wrong.
- A purpose of the form "inform them about X" is a topic. Rewrite it as what they will do or conclude differently afterwards.
- A brief fits on one screen. A long brief is a sign the task has split into several documents.
- For a message under about 200 words, keep only: reader, one takeaway, constraints.
