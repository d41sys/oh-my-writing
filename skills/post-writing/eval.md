# post-writing eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Reader and takeaway

1. Can the one takeaway be stated in a single sentence, and does the draft deliver it?
2. Is it clear who the reader is, and does the opening make sense to that reader without outside context?
3. If the piece wants the reader to act, is there exactly one clear next action, and does it follow from the content?
4. Does every section serve the takeaway, with off-topic material cut or moved to its own post?

## Grounding and honesty

5. Is every anecdote, client, result, quote, metric and credential in the text traceable to the author's supplied material?
6. Are all gaps marked with `[ASK AUTHOR: ...]` or `[VERIFY: ...]` rather than filled with a plausible detail?
7. Are illustrations that are not real events labelled as hypothetical?
8. Do numbers carry their conditions (what was measured, against what, over what period), and are none rounded upward or stripped of a qualifier?
9. Are limitations, trade-offs or failed attempts that matter to the reader still present?
10. Is any fear, urgency or vulnerability in the text real and stated plainly, not staged?

## Opening and title

11. Does the opening lead with an artifact, number, scene or sharp claim, or else carry only the one line of context the reader needs?
12. Does the title or first line promise exactly what the piece delivers, with no gap between promise and content?
13. If the opening raises a question or gap, does the piece answer it?
14. Is the opening free of era or importance throat-clearing ("in today's world", "it's no secret that")?

## Structure and body

15. Does the structure follow the job of the post type (steps, argument, real sequence, announcement facts) rather than a generic template?
16. Does each section do one job, and is that job visible in its heading or first sentence?
17. Is each concept introduced before a later passage depends on it?
18. For technical or abstract points, does the concrete example come before the explanation?
19. In incident and design stories, is the real order kept, with no invented reason for a transition?
20. Is formatting proportional to length, with lists only for parallel items and tables only for repeated fields?
21. Is each adjective of praise either backed by a stated fact or removed?

## Ending

22. Does the piece end on its last concrete point, a real next action or a real open question, rather than a recap, aphorism or stock engagement prompt?
23. If there is a closing question, is it one the author actually wants answered and specific enough to answer?

## Channel fit

24. Does the opening fit the display limit of the target channel (verified, or marked `[VERIFY: limit]`)?
25. Does the format follow the channel's conventions (length, links, tags, paragraphs) without any claim about boosting reach?
26. For long web posts, is the plain answer to the likely query near the top, with descriptive headings and no keyword repetition?
27. For repurposed versions, does each keep the core claim, numbers, qualifiers and the author's voice, and is it restructured, not merely truncated?
28. Does each repurposed version stand alone, with the canonical link included where the full evidence lives?

## Final read

29. Read aloud, does it sound like the author talking to the reader, with no sentence you would not say?
30. Was `anti-slop-editing` run as the last pass if available, or its checks applied, and was the author's voice sample respected over any generic rule?
