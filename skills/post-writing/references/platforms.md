Load when: fitting a post to a specific channel, checking display limits and conventions, or planning a thread, carousel, newsletter or long-form web post.

# Platform formats

**Verify current limits.** Everything in this file is a snapshot of common practice from 2026-10. Character limits, truncation points, image sizes and feature availability change without notice and differ by account type and region. Before depending on a number, check the platform's own help page or the editor itself, or mark it `[VERIFY: limit]` in the draft.

**No distribution claims.** Third-party claims about what a platform's ranking favours (link placement, hashtag counts, posting intervals, reply timing, format weight) are unverified and change often. This file does not use them. Write for a human reader, fit the display limit, and follow the platform's stated rules. If the author asks "what does the algorithm reward", say the guidance is not verifiable and answer with reader-facing craft.

## Universal facts across short-form feeds

- Feeds truncate. The first line or two must stand alone and carry the point or the artifact.
- Many readers are on a phone: short paragraphs (1 to 3 sentences) and visible white space help. Do not turn every sentence into its own line.
- Outside the niche, spell out acronyms once and give the minimum context needed to follow.
- An engagement prompt is fine when the author wants replies and can ask a specific question. Stock prompts ("Thoughts?", "Agree?") add nothing and read as filler.
- Hashtags and mentions: use few, only where the community uses them and where they are genuine. Tagging a person is appropriate only when they are relevant.
- Images, screenshots and charts need alt text where the platform supports it. Recorded video and audio need accurate captions or a transcript; this is an accessibility need, not decoration.
- A post that quotes numbers keeps the conditions with them, even on a short channel (see [repurposing](repurposing.md)).

## LinkedIn

- **Format facts (verify):** feed posts have a character ceiling in the low thousands; the feed shows only the first few lines before a "see more" control; document (PDF) posts display as swipeable pages; polls and images are available.
- **Craft:** the first two lines decide whether the reader expands. Lead with the outcome, the number or the counter-intuitive finding from the author's real work. Then a short body, one takeaway, one clear next step or question if the author wants replies.
- **Typical lengths (heuristic):** practitioner insight posts are often a few hundred words at most; longer pieces fit better as an article or a linked post.
- **Shapes that work when filled with true specifics:**
  - Story: the outcome, the scene, the problem, what was tried, the turn, the result, the lesson, one real question.
  - Contrarian: the claim, the observation or number that changed the view, two or three reasons, the alternative, an invitation to disagree.
  - List: a count and a credibility anchor in the first line, numbered points with one line each, a closing insight.
  - How-to: the outcome, numbered steps with concrete actions, the common failure, one next move.
- **Document or carousel post:** one idea per slide; slide 1 must work alone as the thumbnail; keep a single visual template; end on one action. The text above the document is a second hook; write it as a standalone post, not "see attached". Judge a carousel by whether readers reach the end, and fix slide 1 or the framework before the middle.
- **Avoid:** performative vulnerability, fake founder reflection, praise-stacking, corporate speak with no person behind it, a single-sentence-per-line "poem" format used for drama rather than readability, and stock openers ("Excited to share", "Here's what I learned") that postpone the point.
- **Links:** put the link where the reader would naturally want it. Some authors place it in a comment instead; treat that as a style choice to verify, not a reach tactic.

## X (long and short posts, threads)

- **Format facts (verify):** the standard limit is 280 characters; some account types can write longer posts; threads chain posts; images, video and polls are supported.
- **Craft:** lead with a specific claim or artifact in the first words; name the topic early with concrete nouns (tools, companies, numbers) rather than vague ones. A standalone lead post should deliver value even if nobody opens the thread.
- **Thread rules:** one claim per post; each post makes sense when read alone; number the posts if the thread is long; put the strongest evidence in the middle, not only in the first post; end with the link or the next action.
- **Thread shapes:**
  - Tutorial: promise, steps, the gotcha, the result.
  - Story: stakes, sequence, turn, lesson (often 8 to 12 posts).
  - Breakdown: claim, mechanism pieces, implication.
- **Use a thread only when one post would collapse the argument.** Otherwise write one tight post.
- **Quote-posting:** add a specific point the original did not make; a bare restatement adds nothing.
- **Avoid:** "like and retweet if...", chains of "No X, no Y, no Z", self-answered questions ("The result? 3x faster."), padding to reach a post count.

## Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon and other short-text networks

- **Format facts (verify):** per-post limits differ: Threads is in the 500-character range, Bluesky is around 300 characters, Mastodon defaults to about 500 and varies by server. Check the current value for the instance.
- **Craft:** compress to the sharpest claim or artifact; keep the author's cadence; avoid generic filler; do not paste a shortened LinkedIn post. Hashtags are optional and community-dependent; on some servers they matter for discovery, on others they read as noise.
- **Threads:** the same discipline as X threads: each post must stand alone.

## Instagram

- **Format facts (verify):** captions have a character ceiling in the low thousands; only the first line or two shows before "more"; links in captions may not be clickable; carousels allow many slides; recommended image ratios and sizes change.
- **Craft:** the image or first slide must work without the caption; the caption's first line stands alone; carousel slides carry one idea each; a closing slide carries one action.
- **Photo carousel skeleton:** slide 1 a bold statement or question; slides 2 to 9 one point each; slide 10 a summary and one action; the caption expands and adds context.
- **Short video:** the first second or two decide whether the viewer stays. Plan three tracks (what is seen, what is said, on-screen text) so they complement each other instead of repeating, and add captions.

## Carousel and slide frameworks (any platform)

Pick by the job of the content. Counts are typical, not rules; a good piece uses as many slides as it has distinct points.

| Content job | Framework | Typical slides |
|---|---|---|
| A list of resources, tools or tips | Value stack: cover states count and subject; one item per slide | 4 to 14 |
| A real result with a system behind it | Problem and proof: number first, reframe the problem, steps and tools, receipt | 6 to 10 |
| Several named techniques on one theme | Hack list: common approach fails, then one named technique per slide with before and after | 6 to 10 |
| A firm opinion on a common practice | Callout: claim, specific detail that shows the issue, fairness pivot, signed close | 4 to 8 |
| A product or workflow you can show | Demo walkthrough: finished result, the problem, numbered steps, result | 5 to 11 |
| An engineering explainer | Topic and outcome, problem context, labelled diagram or table, implementation takeaways, reference slide | 5 to 10 |

- The count on the cover must match the slides. Do not pad.
- Numbers, screenshots and results must be the author's own and real.
- Keep text legible at thumbnail size and limit text per slide to what one idea needs.
- Where the author must supply an image, write `[ASK AUTHOR: image: what it shows]`; do not invent screenshots or data.

## Forums and communities (Reddit, Hacker News, Stack-style communities, Discord)

- **Format facts (verify):** title limits and rules differ: Reddit titles allow a few hundred characters; Hacker News titles are short (around 80 characters). Each community has its own self-promotion rules; read them first.
- **Craft:** a descriptive, value-first title with no clickbait; share the insight, not just the product. Body: context for why it matters here, what was tried or learned, what happened, what the author would do differently, an optional link, a genuine question. Show real numbers and conditions.
- **Avoid:** upvote requests, "check out my tool", hype, emoji or hashtags in titles where the community dislikes them. Disclose the author's relationship to the thing being posted.
- **Launch posts:** state what it is and what is different; say what feedback is wanted; answer comments promptly and honestly.

## Newsletter and email

- **Scope:** newsletters and email sequences are written here. Personal outreach emails for jobs or academia (cold, networking, referral, prospective supervisor) are `career-documents`; investor, customer or sales outreach is not covered by these skills.
- **Format facts (verify):** subject lines are cut off on phones after a few dozen characters (a heuristic of roughly 40 to 60); preview text follows the subject; images may be blocked by default.
- **Craft:** see the newsletter section in [post types](post-types.md) and, for series and team newsletters, [email sequences](email-sequences.md). The subject line must work if it is the only thing the reader sees and must be true. The first screen carries substance. Single column, short paragraphs, descriptive anchor text, one primary action, a human sign-off.
- **Product update email:** what is new in plain words, why it matters as an outcome, how to use it with a direct link.
- **Roundup:** curated links with the author's commentary on why each matters; a list of headlines alone adds nothing.
- **Legal and deliverability:** unsubscribe route, sender identity and consent rules vary by region. Mark `[VERIFY: applicable email rules]` and leave specifics to the sending platform.

## Blog and long-form article

- **Format facts (verify):** most platforms use Markdown or a rich editor; code blocks with language tags; image alt text; many support a separate meta description (a common range is around 150 to 160 characters) and a canonical link.
- **Length:** match the job. A focused technical post often runs one to three thousand words; shorter is fine when the takeaway is small. Do not pad to a target.
- **Structure:** a title that says what the piece is about with the terms a reader would search; an opening that gives the answer or the artifact; descriptive headings that state the point of each section rather than a generic label ("Handling lock contention under load" instead of "Background"); a table for repeated fields; code blocks that run as written.
- **Search and answer engines:** the durable practice is clarity. State the answer to the likely question in plain words early; define a term at first use; under a question-shaped heading, begin with the direct answer, then the nuance; use tables with labelled units; state versions, dates and environments; keep dated facts current. Write one version for people and machines. Do not repeat a keyword for its own sake, do not split a coherent piece into fragments for machines, and do not publish near-identical pages. Reported citation-uplift numbers for particular tactics are unverified; do not promise them.
- **Match the page to the query's intent.** A reader asking "how does X work" wants an explanation; "X vs Y" wants a comparison with stated criteria; "where is X" wants the link or location; "get X" wants the steps or the download. Give each page one primary subject, and do not publish several near-identical pages for the same query. Put the subject terms in the title, the first heading and the opening, in the words the reader uses; read the sentence aloud and rewrite any that sounds forced. Cover the follow-up questions a reader would ask next in the same piece, as real sections. Use an FAQ block only for questions readers actually ask.
- **Video and audio.** Search and answer systems read the text around a recording: the transcript, captions, title, chapters and description. Say names and key answers aloud, not only on a slide; correct the captions; title each chapter by the question it answers; put a short written answer in the description. This is accessibility and clarity work, not a ranking trick.
- **Authorship:** a visible author and date, first-hand experience stated where true, and sources for claims make a piece more credible to readers and are what a careful search system also looks for. Do not invent credentials.
- **Canonical copy:** publish the full version on a place the author controls, then point from shorter posts back to it (see [repurposing](repurposing.md)).

## WeChat official account (公众号)

- **Format facts (verify):** long-form articles read on mobile; a title, a cover image and a summary field; tables render poorly, so keep them simple or use images.
- **Craft:** a short opening summary, short paragraphs, headings the reader can skim, comparative tables only where simple, a professional but conversational tone. Typical heuristic: roughly 1,200 to 1,800 Chinese characters.
- **Language:** keep technical terms as the community writes them (often in English). Rewrite rather than translate an English source; check for formulaic openers in the target language.

## Xiaohongshu (RED)

- **Format facts (verify):** visual-first notes; a short title (commonly under about 20 characters shows well), images with text, tags.
- **Craft:** practical value, authentic experience and strong images. Three common types: a recommendation with a real result, a how-to, and an experience reflection. A first-person opening with a specific detail; short paragraphs; emoji as occasional visual anchors, not decoration on every line; bold labels for scannable points; a genuine limitation named; an actionable takeaway; tags.
- **Image guidance:** where the author must supply the cover or content images (a labelled result screenshot, a before and after), write `[ASK AUTHOR: 配图: what it shows]`.
- **Avoid:** academic openers, ad-copy tone, praise with no reason, titles the note does not deliver, forced slang, invented usage durations. Commands and settings must stay exact.

## Choosing channels (planning note)

- Pick one or two channels where the audience already reads. Using many with no central piece spreads effort without building on itself.
- A channel the author owns (a blog, a newsletter, a repository) lasts; rented feeds are fast but their rules and reach change. Use feeds to point at the owned piece.
- Posts decay at different speeds by channel (minutes to hours on fast feeds, about a day on most social feeds, much longer for blog and search). Do not repost a fast-feed item as if it were evergreen, and do not expect an evergreen post to behave like a feed post.
- These timings are snapshot heuristics; check the author's own analytics rather than assuming.
