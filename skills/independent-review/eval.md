# independent-review eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Independence

1. Was every review round run in a new session, subagent or process, rather than in the writer's own context?
2. If no fresh context was available, does the report say "Independent review: not run" with the reason, and label anything you did yourself as a self-check, instead of presenting it as a review?
3. Does a self-check avoid a verdict of `ready` or "this is good" in the writer's own voice, list what it cannot see, and name what only the author can confirm (a date with no year or weekday, a place or number the brief leaves open)?
4. Where a different model family was possible, was it used, and does the report give the reviewer's reported model id (or "unknown") and whether it matches the writer's family?
5. Does the packet sent to the reviewer contain only the artifact, the reader brief, hard constraints, sources, an optional rubric, the reading date (when the text has yearless or relative dates), and the reader's own questions (three to five things anyone in that role needs answered before acting)?
6. Is the packet free of the writer's intent, hoped-for conclusion, requested verdict, worries, drafting history, earlier versions, and warnings about weak spots, including reader questions that name the draft's known gaps?
7. Is the packet free of any statement of what was fixed or changed since an earlier round?
8. Are the review questions open ("what is the main point?") rather than leading ("isn't this clear?")?
9. Are sources passed as files or raw material, not as your summary of them?
10. Was it checked that the text may leave the session (confidential, unpublished, client or personal material), and was the owner asked when unclear?

## Fresh-reader test

11. Did you write the expected main point and expected reader questions before reading the reader's reply?
12. Is the reader's restated main point compared with the brief and labelled matches, partly, or misses?
13. Is every stall classified as text defect, audience boundary, reader limitation, or out of scope, with only text defects fixed?
14. Were test questions written without containing their own answers?

## Findings and triage

15. In a review report, does every finding carry a severity, a location (quote or section), and a reader consequence? (A triage-only table, triage section 8, carries severity only where you changed it.)
16. Where severity is given, is it assigned by reader impact rather than reviewer tone, and are taste-only items marked nit?
17. Does every finding have exactly one decision: accept, reject with a one-line evidence-based reason, or needs author?
18. Was any finding that would add an unsupported claim, number, quote, name or example rejected as written, and turned into a needs-author question in the findings (not text in the artifact) if the gap is real?
19. Are accepted fixes the smallest edits, with the author's voice, claims, qualifiers, numbers and structure intact, reported as a list or marked edits? Is every revised draft or rewritten passage free of `[ASK AUTHOR]`, `[VERIFY]` and `[UNTESTED]` markers, review notes, finding IDs and process narration, with gaps left as they were, dropped, or stated plainly, each question put in the findings, and no heading (except a notice's own short headline), signposting, closing recap paragraph or repeated caveat added to the author's text?
20. Were fixes applied in dependency order and diffed against the version the reviewer saw for drift?
21. Was a suggestion that changes the document's purpose or audience named as drift and sent to the author, not folded in as a fix?
22. Where a finding was contested, was a separate verification job used and labelled, with the evidence given as locations the reviewer re-reads, and is no fabricated evidence used in the rebuttal?

## Calibration and comparison

23. For subjective criteria, does the report state calibration status, and say `calibration: none` rather than inventing anchors?
24. Were any good/bad anchor examples chosen by a human, and did the sanity test show the bad examples scoring below the good ones?
25. Is a same-family or self-score used only to rank fixes, never to say the text is ready?
26. Are reviewer disagreements explained (cause and action) instead of averaged?
27. If two versions were compared, were labels anonymous, order randomized, and the comparison swapped or reported as order-sensitive?

## Responding to reviewer comments (only when a reply is the deliverable)

28. Was the venue or client contract (format, limit, anonymity, new-data policy, deadline) collected, with unknowns recorded as `[VERIFY: ...]` in the hand-off rather than quoted from memory in the letter?
29. Are the comments split into single issues with stable IDs, each anchored in a verbatim quote that was not edited?
30. Does every material issue have a block with a direct first-sentence answer, evidence, a change location (if revisions are allowed), and a remaining limit?
31. Is every fact in the reply traceable to the manuscript, the review, or something the author confirmed, and is every promise either done, approved by the author, or labelled future work? Is the letter itself free of markers and notes to the author, with those questions in the hand-off?
32. Does every issue end answered, deferred with a reason, or needs author, with none dropped?
33. Does the reply invent no experiment, number, citation or participant quote, and switch to a concession or future-work line when evidence is missing?
34. Do quotes from the manuscript match the revised text word for word, and do table, figure and section numbers match?

## Stopping and reporting

35. Does the review stop within two rounds, or is the extra round approved by the user?
36. Does the report say which parts of the artifact the reviewer read, and is a reply that was cut off, covered only part of it, or gave a bare `ready` with no findings or scores treated as no review, rerun, and never reported as a pass?
37. Is the "edit, re-review, repeat until clean" loop absent?
38. Are unresolved critical or major items listed under Remaining, with the reason and the effort needed?
39. Where the author might assume the review covered something it cannot (technical correctness, legal or medical accuracy), does the report say so once and route it to a specialist or the author?
40. Is the depth (light, standard, high) stated and proportionate to the stakes, with the number of sessions used?
41. Is the report short, with changes listed one line each, each point said once (not again in a verdict, remaining or not-covered line), no claims of verification you did not perform, and each question to the author a plain sentence naming the missing item (no marker syntax unless the question sits in a file the author will edit)?
42. When the report or triage is saved as a file, does the chat reply name the file, the decision and the one open question, without repeating the file?
43. When you wrote the text you self-checked, did you apply what the check found (restated facts, stock thanks, a closing paragraph that repeats the body) before handing it over?

## Final read

Read the report once as the author. Does it say what to do next, what was rejected and why, and what only the author can answer? If a sentence would not change what the author does, cut it. Then read any revised text or letter once as its real reader: is there a marker, a note to the author or a mention of the review process left in it? Remove it.
