# independent-review eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Independence

1. Was every review round run in a new session, subagent or process, rather than in the writer's own context?
2. If no fresh context was available, does the report say "Independent review: not run" with the reason, and label anything you did yourself as a self-check, instead of presenting it as a review?
3. Does a self-check avoid a verdict of `ready` or "this is good" in the writer's own voice, and list what it cannot see?
4. Where a different model family was possible, was it used, and does the report give the reviewer's reported model id (or "unknown") and whether it matches the writer's family?
5. Does the packet sent to the reviewer contain only the artifact, the reader brief, hard constraints, sources, and an optional rubric?
6. Is the packet free of the writer's intent, hoped-for conclusion, requested verdict, drafting history, earlier versions, and warnings about weak spots?
7. Is the packet free of any statement of what was fixed or changed since an earlier round?
8. Are the review questions open ("what is the main point?") rather than leading ("isn't this clear?")?
9. Are sources passed as files or raw material, not as your summary of them?
10. Was it checked that the text may leave the session (confidential, unpublished, client or personal material), and was the owner asked when unclear?

## Fresh-reader test

11. Did you write the expected main point and expected reader questions before reading the reader's reply?
12. Is the reader's restated main point compared with the brief and labelled matches, partly, or misses?
13. Is every stall classified as text defect, audience boundary, reader limitation, or out of scope, with only text defects fixed?
14. Were test questions written without containing their own answers?

## Findings and triage

15. Does every finding carry a severity, a location (quote or section), and a reader consequence?
16. Is severity assigned by reader impact rather than reviewer tone, and are taste-only items marked nit?
17. Does every finding have exactly one decision: accept, reject with a one-line evidence-based reason, or needs author?
18. Was any finding that would add an unsupported claim, number, quote, name or example rejected as written, and turned into `[ASK AUTHOR: ...]` if the gap is real?
19. Are accepted fixes the smallest edits, with the author's voice, claims, qualifiers, numbers and structure intact, reported as a list or marked edits, with no review notes left in the artifact?
20. Were fixes applied in dependency order and diffed against the version the reviewer saw for drift?
21. Was a suggestion that changes the document's purpose or audience named as drift and sent to the author, not folded in as a fix?
22. Where a finding was contested, was a separate verification job used and labelled, with the evidence given as locations the reviewer re-reads, and is no fabricated evidence used in the rebuttal?

## Calibration and comparison

23. For subjective criteria, does the report state calibration status, and say `calibration: none` rather than inventing anchors?
24. Were any good/bad anchor examples chosen by a human, and did the sanity test show the bad examples scoring below the good ones?
25. Is a same-family or self-score used only to rank fixes, never to say the text is ready?
26. Are reviewer disagreements explained (cause and action) instead of averaged?
27. If two versions were compared, were labels anonymous, order randomized, and the comparison swapped or reported as order-sensitive?

## Responding to reviewer comments (only when a reply is the deliverable)

28. Was the venue or client contract (format, limit, anonymity, new-data policy, deadline) collected, with unknowns marked `[VERIFY: ...]` rather than quoted from memory?
29. Are the comments split into single issues with stable IDs, each anchored in a verbatim quote that was not edited?
30. Does every material issue have a block with a direct first-sentence answer, evidence, a change location (if revisions are allowed), and a remaining limit?
31. Is every fact in the reply traceable to the manuscript, the review, or something the author confirmed, and is every promise either done, approved by the author, or labelled future work?
32. Does every issue end answered, deferred with a reason, or needs author, with none dropped?
33. Does the reply invent no experiment, number, citation or participant quote, and switch to a concession or future-work line when evidence is missing?
34. Do quotes from the manuscript match the revised text word for word, and do table, figure and section numbers match?

## Stopping and reporting

35. Does the review stop within two rounds, or is the extra round approved by the user?
36. Is the "edit, re-review, repeat until clean" loop absent?
37. Are unresolved critical or major items listed under Remaining, with the reason and the effort needed?
38. Does the report say what the review does not cover (for example technical correctness, legal or medical accuracy), and route those to a specialist or the author?
39. Is the depth (light, standard, high) stated and proportionate to the stakes, with the number of sessions used?
40. Is the report short, with changes listed one line each, and no claims of verification you did not perform?

## Final read

Read the report once as the author. Does it say what to do next, what was rejected and why, and what only the author can answer? If a sentence would not change what the author does, cut it.
