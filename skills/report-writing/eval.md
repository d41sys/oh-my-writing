# report-writing eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Purpose and lead

1. Does the report name the decision, the decision-maker, or the record's reader, and does the shape fit that job?
2. Do the first lines give the recommendation or finding (or, when the notes leave the choice open, say so and give the direction the evidence supports), not background or a description of the report?
3. Is the confidence stated with the reason it rests on, not just a word?
4. Does the lead say what is requested and by when, and, where the author stated a recommendation, add no gate, trial, fallback or deadline to it and no first-person promise the notes do not make?
5. Could the core finding be disputed by a knowledgeable reader, and did it survive the strongest counter-evidence in the material?

## Evidence and claims

6. Does every number carry unit, period, scope, baseline, source, and as-of date (or does the report state plainly that the notes do not give the missing item)?
7. Are observation, inference, forecast, and recommendation distinguishable from each other on the page?
8. Are estimates, vendor claims, and forecasts labeled as such, with the assumptions that drive them visible?
9. Does every figure, name, quote, and source come from the author's material or a cited source, with nothing invented, and is every source named rather than "studies show"? Does every number, threshold, config value, and duration in the notes still appear, in plain text in the notes' own form (40, not "forty"; 30 seconds, not `30 seconds`; a sentence is reworded rather than opened with a spelled-out number), or is each deliberate omission listed in the reply?
10. Is the deliverable free of `[ASK AUTHOR]`, `[VERIFY]`, `[UNTESTED]` markers, draft banners, and meta-notes (process narration, "as requested", `[source: ...]` tags, per-paragraph source notes)? Is each gap stated in reader language or the sentence omitted, with nothing invented to fill it, and does each question appear as a marker line in the reply (and in `open-questions.md` when a file was saved)? Exception: the author asked for a draft to edit.
11. Were totals, percentages, growth rates, and conversions recomputed from their parts, do they agree, and is each derived figure labeled a calculation rather than passed off as a source's number? Where the notes give a rate and a volume, is the cost over the budget period shown (rate x volume x days, both totals and the gap) instead of only a per-unit gap?
12. Do figures in this report match the deck, model, memo, or earlier version they must agree with, including rounding?

## Comparisons and options

13. Are metrics, time windows, baselines, and scope the same across every candidate, or is each mismatch stated beside the figure?
14. Where windows or definitions could not be aligned, does the report refuse to rank, sum, or average on that criterion, show the figures side by side with each window named, and say what measurement would make them comparable?
15. Were criteria and weights stated as choices before scores, with the ranking shown under at least one alternative weighting?
16. Do tension poles and failed hard requirements stay visible instead of being averaged into a total?
17. Is each key claim about a candidate tagged proven, asserted, or unknown, and is unknown never presented as no?
18. Is each matrix row followed by a line of interpretation, and is the candidate set justified, with the status quo or a fallback named only where the notes describe it?
19. Does the recommendation give action, expected impact (qualitative if unmeasured), effort or cost, and, when the notes give one, the condition that would reverse it, with no invented trial, gate or deadline?
20. Is the recommendation the author's own, either stated in the notes or confirmed? Where the notes gave none, does the document open with the direction the evidence supports, labeled as the writer's reading and not the author's decision (or say it favors neither), say once that no recommendation is made, give each option's cost from the notes, give a decision rule from the notes' own options and deadlines (what settles it, what to do on pass, on fail or if the evidence cannot be had in time), invent no test design, budget, owner or date, and keep the question in the reply instead of a marker in the document?

## Structure, visuals, and voice

21. Does the executive summary stand alone, contain no method, and introduce no figure or claim missing from the body (and does a short report have none, with its opening paragraph doing that job)?
22. Is each table or figure chosen for the relationship it shows, with a caption that states the finding, and does the report still make sense without the figures?
23. Is color never the only carrier of meaning, and is missing data shown as missing, not zero?
24. Is the register right for the type (cool and active for decisions, terse for status, plain for investors and post-mortems) with no hype or empty adjectives carrying weight, no headings in a text under about 400 words or in a message, no framework labels the author would not write ("Confidence: medium", "Decision rule"), no announced "my confidence is moderate" (the reason sits in the claim), no "according to the notes" prefaces on flatly stated facts, and the author's own person (no third-person narration about the author)?
25. Is hedging confined to the claims that are genuinely uncertain, with no blanket qualifiers (no "may", "roughly" or "appears" on a figure the notes state exactly), is each caveat or unknown stated once at its claim instead of again in the summary, the body and the close, and is a figure joined to its context by a comma, parentheses or a sentence rather than paired em dashes?
26. For a status report: does the first line give health, trend, the biggest blocker or decision needed, and what happens next, and does each decision carry its date and the cost of a slip?
27. For a post-mortem: does it open with what customers experienced (window, duration, count, peak rate), is it blameless (roles and conditions, no name beside a verdict), are causes traced to mechanisms with evidence, are times and durations the author's own or computed from given timestamps, and do action items name the specific change, an owner, a date, and how success will be seen?
28. For an audit report: does it lead with what failed and the verdict, and does it separate "could not check" from "broken"?
29. For investor material: do assumptions show, do sums add exactly, and is it free of legal, financial, or compliance conclusions?

## Editing and hand-off

30. In revise mode, are the author's claims, qualifiers, numbers, names, and structure preserved except where they were the defect, and is every change listed with its reason?
31. For non-English text, were structural checks applied and English word lists left unused?
32. Did claim checks go to `evidence-grounded-writing` and the final pass to `anti-slop-editing` if available, or were the checks above applied directly?

## Final read

Read only the first paragraph and the last paragraph as the decision-maker would. Can they say what is recommended, how sure the author is, and what they must do next? Is there anything they would find, after a minute's checking, to be wrong or unsupported? If either answer is no, fix it before returning.
