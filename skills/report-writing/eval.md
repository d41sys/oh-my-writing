# report-writing eval

Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every failure before returning.

## Purpose and lead

1. Does the report name the decision, the decision-maker, or the record's reader, and does the shape fit that job?
2. Do the first lines give the recommendation or finding, not background or a description of the report?
3. Is the confidence stated with the reason it rests on, not just a word?
4. Does the lead say what would change the recommendation, or what is requested and by when?
5. Could the core finding be disputed by a knowledgeable reader, and did it survive the strongest counter-evidence in the material?

## Evidence and claims

6. Does every number carry unit, period, scope, baseline, source, and as-of date (or an explicit marker where one is missing)?
7. Are observation, inference, forecast, and recommendation distinguishable from each other on the page?
8. Are estimates, vendor claims, and forecasts labeled as such, with the assumptions that drive them visible?
9. Does every figure, name, quote, and source come from the author's material or a cited source, with nothing invented?
10. Are gaps marked as `[ASK AUTHOR: ...]` or `[VERIFY: ...]`, and is the draft banner present if any remain?
11. Were totals, percentages, growth rates, and conversions recomputed from their parts, and do they agree?
12. Do figures in this report match the deck, model, memo, or earlier version they must agree with, including rounding?

## Comparisons and options

13. Are metrics, time windows, baselines, and scope the same across every candidate, or is each mismatch stated beside the figure?
14. Were criteria and weights stated as choices before scores, with the ranking shown under at least one alternative weighting?
15. Do tension poles and failed hard requirements stay visible instead of being averaged into a total?
16. Is each key claim about a candidate tagged proven, asserted, or unknown, and is unknown never presented as no?
17. Is each matrix row followed by a line of interpretation, and is the candidate set justified, including the status quo?
18. Does the recommendation give action, expected impact (qualitative if unmeasured), effort or cost, and a condition that would reverse it?

## Structure, visuals, and voice

19. Does the executive summary stand alone, contain no method, and introduce no figure or claim missing from the body?
20. Is each table or figure chosen for the relationship it shows, with a caption that states the finding, and does the report still make sense without the figures?
21. Is color never the only carrier of meaning, and is missing data shown as missing, not zero?
22. Is the register right for the type (cool and active for decisions, terse for status, plain for investors and post-mortems) with no hype or empty adjectives carrying weight?
23. Is hedging confined to the claims that are genuinely uncertain, with no blanket qualifiers?
24. For a post-mortem: is it blameless, are causes traced to mechanisms with evidence, and do action items have owners and a way to verify them?
25. For investor material: do assumptions show, do sums add exactly, and is it free of legal, financial, or compliance conclusions?

## Editing and hand-off

26. In revise mode, are the author's claims, qualifiers, numbers, names, and structure preserved except where they were the defect, and is every change listed with its reason?
27. For non-English text, were structural checks applied and English word lists left unused?
28. Did claim checks go to `evidence-grounded-writing` and the final pass to `anti-slop-editing` if available, or were the checks above applied directly?

## Final read

Read only the first paragraph and the last paragraph as the decision-maker would. Can they say what is recommended, how sure the author is, and what they must do next? Is there anything they would find, after a minute's checking, to be wrong or unsupported? If either answer is no, fix it before returning.
